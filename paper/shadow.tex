\begin{abstract}

Anticipated third-party intervention shapes the decision to rebel, yet
no measure covers the full population of potential interveners.  I construct one: a machine-learning ensemble
predicts intervention probability and direction for every dyad-year
(1946--2014); a fixed point makes them mutually consistent; their
sum is the country-year shadow.  Added to a standard onset model, it
improves out-of-sample prediction, sharply once resolved by intervener
type, and encompasses the leading measures, each limited to one side of
intervention or the major powers.  In one bargaining framework, three
frictions stake predictions on its two axes.  Onset falls with the
intensity and rises with the tilt toward the
opposition---a pattern easier to square with limited commitment or
divergent expectations than with the standard screen.
\end{abstract}
